# Sources

Every reference used in the in-app notes, the claims it supports, and where it is cited.
This file is generated from `src/aguzaderas/sources.js` and `src/aguzaderas/notes.js` by
`npm run sources`; edit those files rather than this one. The research behind them, with a
confidence mark on every claim and the sources that could not be reached, is in
`docs/research/aguzaderas.md`.

The Castillo de las Aguzaderas is a real castle at El Coronil (Seville). The model follows the
published descriptions and a plan measured from OpenStreetMap and the PNOA orthophoto. No
measured plan or section was available online, so heights and wall thicknesses are estimates.
Nothing is recorded of the garrison; the people and furnishings are conjecture and are marked so.

## 1. IAPH, Guía Digital

Instituto Andaluz del Patrimonio Histórico, *Guía Digital del Patrimonio Cultural de Andalucía*: "Castillo de las Aguzaderas" (code 01410360043), accessed October 2026. <https://guiadigital.iaph.es/bien/inmueble/19246/sevilla/el-coronil/castillo-de-las-aguzaderas>

Supports:

- A near-square plan with square corner towers, two semicircular towers on opposite flanks and a square keep (its compass bearings are turned a quarter from the ground; see the research notes).
- Corner towers solid up to the wall-walk, then one chamber under a pointed barrel vault with arrow slits on all four faces and a hatch to the roof terrace, reached by wooden ladders; the wall-walk the only way between towers, reached by a stair against the curtain.
- The keep: two storeys each under a sail vault (bóveda vaída), ashlar at its corners, plinth and door, and bartizans on corbels at the corners of its terrace, of which stubs remain.
- At the fourth corner, a square tower joined to the circuit by a pointed arch: once an albarrana tower enclosing a well, later surrounded by a quadrangular enclosure (early 15th century, with a query) that turned the arch into its gate.
- The place appears as "Aznalcázar" by the Aguzaderas spring in the Repartimiento of Seville and was given to the cathedral chapter; works 1348–1355; taken by the Crown in 1355 for 6,000 maravedís a year; granted to Per Afán de Ribera in 1388; later held by the House of Medinaceli.
- Later used as a soap works and then a cattle pen.

Cited in: About; Room: keep; Room: keepTerrace; Room: cornerTower; Room: albarrana; Room: annex; Room: wallWalk

## 2. Wikipedia (ES), "Castillo de las Aguzaderas"

"Castillo de las Aguzaderas", *Wikipedia* (Spanish), accessed October 2026. <https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castillo_de_las_Aguzaderas>

Supports:

- The castle stands in a hollow rather than on a height, because it was built to guard the spring; a ring of watchtowers (Cote, El Bollo, Lopera, Águila, Alocaz and others) kept the wider watch.
- Its people moved to El Coronil in the 15th century; the castle was restored in the 1960s.
- Protected as a monument (now a Bien de Interés Cultural).

Cited in: About; Room: spring

## 4. La Casa de la Tercia, 2016

La Casa de la Tercia (blog), "El Coronil: Castillo de las Aguzaderas", June 2016. <http://lacasadelatercia.blogspot.com/2016/06/el-coronil-castillo-de-las-aguzaderas.html>

Supports:

- The keep in the middle of the south wall, projecting beyond it; the semicircular towers on the east and west walls; rubble walling with dressed corners.
- The gate in the west wall: a pointed arch with the remains of a machicolation above.
- The keep door with a machicolation over it; inside, a vaulted room with a small annexe overlooked from the upper floor through a window and a murder hole; a narrow stair to the terrace.

- The albarrana tower protected the spring, and a wall without a parapet was later built round it; a blocked arch under it.

## 6. Molina Rozalem, "Torre Lopera"

Molina Rozalem, "Una fortaleza en la frontera: la torre Lopera", University of Seville (chapter, 2018). <https://grupo.us.es/tutsosmodhum/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/Cap%C3%ADtulo-libro.-Una-fortaleza-en-la-frontera.pdf>

Supports:

- The keep of Las Aguzaderas belongs to a family of Seville council towers of the early 14th century (Águila, Lopera, Gandul), nearly identical in design.
- It stands on a rubble podium about 1 m high; its door is of ashlar, the lintel carried on corbels with a double roll moulding.
- Torre Lopera, the closest published analogue: 12.9 × 11.3 m, walls 2.3–2.4 m thick with a thicker wall for the stair, two vaulted chambers and a terrace.
- Frontier towers sheltered settlers and their herds.

Cited in: About; Room: keep; Room: court

The towers and merlons were heavily restored in the 1960s, and the merlons’ medieval shape is not certain.
- **About**: Nothing is recorded of the garrison: not the alcaide’s name, nor its numbers, nor how it lived. The people, the fire in the courtyard and the furnishings follow what is known of frontier garrisons generally, and all are *(conjecture)*. Where the sources disagree, as they do on the gate, the notes say so.
- **Room: keep**: It has two storeys, each a single chamber under a sail vault (bóveda vaída): a shallow dome rising from the four corners. The door, of ashlar, has its lintel on corbels moulded with a double roll, and a machicolation above it at the level of the upper floor. Inside, a small annexe by the entrance is overlooked from above through a window and a murder hole (buhera); here the murder hole opens through the vault over the entrance *(conjecture)*.
