# Aberwen Castle, c. 1290

**An interactive ink-and-watercolour cutaway of a castle that never existed, built the way Edward I's castles in North Wales were.**

![The castle cut in half along the gate passage, the well and the great hall, with fires lit in the kitchens](docs/images/overview.jpg)

Aberwen is a fictional concentric castle modelled on Harlech, Conwy and Beaumaris. It is sliced clean through the gate passage, the well and the great hall, so you look into it like the drawing in a guidebook. The castle is alive inside: on a grey late-autumn morning before dinner, the household is at work in the kitchens, stores and wards. You can orbit it, fly into any room, hover over anything for its proper name, and work the machinery yourself.

Everything is drawn in code: the masonry, the people, the hens and the paper texture. There are no models or image assets.

A second, real castle sits alongside it: the **Castillo de las Aguzaderas** at El Coronil (Seville), as it may have stood about 1410. Switch between the two with the buttons at the top of the room list (see [below](#a-real-castle-las-aguzaderas-c-1410)).

### The service rooms in full detail

The kitchen, bakehouse, brewhouse, larder, pantry, buttery, cellar, well and store-house are fully furnished. They are stocked with what a 1290 household actually ate and drank. There are no potatoes, no hops and no turkeys.

### The towers: the king's lodging and the garrison

The two northern corner towers are cut open to show a chamber on every floor.

- **The king's tower** (north-west): a prison at the bottom with a prisoner in fetters and his gaoler; the household knights' chamber; the king's privy chamber, painted green with gold stars, where the king sits in his chair of state under a cloth of his arms while a messenger kneels, a clerk reads, a lady sews and a minstrel plays; and at the top the king's bedchamber, with a great bed hung in red and gold and a page asleep at its foot.
- **The garrison tower** (north-east): the armoury, with racks of spears, bows, crossbows, mail and helmets, a grindstone throwing sparks and a fletcher at his bench; a dormitory of straw pallets where the men off watch sleep; their mess, where they play at dice; and the constable's chamber with the garrison's pay chest.

### Stable and home farm

A stable against the north outer curtain holds the horses, with grooms mucking out and filling the hay racks. Beyond the ditch is a small home farm: a byre and paddock with cows and a dairymaid milking, a pigsty with a sow and her litter, geese on a pond, goats on the rocks, a dovecote with doves wheeling round it, and the butts where archers practise.

### People and animals at work

There are well over a hundred figures and animals, each running its own routine:

- **Kitchen and stores:** the cook, scullions, the spit boy, the baker, the brewer, the butler and pantler, the water carrier, and a carter unloading grain while a clerk notches the tally.
- **The king's household:** the king, a messenger, a chancery clerk, a lady, a minstrel, the chamberlain, a page, knights and a squire.
- **The garrison:** watchmen and a crossbowman on the walls, guards at the inner gate, a sergeant drilling spearmen in the ward, archers at the butts, the armourer, the fletcher, men asleep and at dice, and the constable at his desk.
- **The wards:** a smith at the forge, masons and sawyers, a gardener, a laundress, a henwife, the porter, grooms, and children chasing each other round the ward.
- **Animals:** horses, cows, pigs and piglets, goats, geese, hens, hounds, a cat stalking rats in the larder, sheep with a shepherd's boy on the hill, and doves.
- **Overhead:** jackdaws wheel round the towers and the banners stream in the wind.

People walk with a fuller stride (the pelvis turns, the shoulders swing against it, knees fold through the step), ease from one action into the next, turn on the spot before setting off, and slow at the end of a walk. They give way to one another: someone in the way is passed on the right, and a walker slows behind a person who blocks the path. People at work glance up at passers-by, and hens and geese scatter from anyone walking through them.

### Say good day to anyone

Click a person and they stop and greet you: a bow, a curtsey, a soldier's fist to the breast, or a wave. People sitting at work just look up. The notes panel then shows what they are doing at that moment, and **Follow** keeps the camera on them as they go about their day.

### Seven working mechanisms

| Mechanism | What you can do |
|---|---|
| **Alarm bell and portcullises** | Ring the bell on the gatehouse roof: the garrison turns out, the archers run in from the butts, the watch stands to, and the three portcullises drop. Stand down and everyone goes back to work. |
| **The butts** | Call a volley, or draw a bow yourself: hold to draw and let go to loose. Under-draw and it falls short; hold it too long and it flies high. |
| **Well windlass** | Drag the crank to lower the bucket 24 m. Wind it up full and tip it into the trough. |
| **Roasting spit** | Stop the spit boy and watch one side char, then turn it yourself to brown it evenly. |
| **Bread oven** | Fire it, rake out the embers, swab it, set the loaves with the peel, seal it, bake and draw. |
| **Cellar trapdoor and hoist** | Open the hatch in the hall floor and wind a cask up from the undercroft. |
| **Buttery tap** | Open the faucet with the vent peg shut and the ale glugs and stops. |

### Firelight and the time of day

<img src="docs/images/dusk.jpg" alt="The castle from the north at dusk, dark against a warm sky, smoke rising from the chimneys" width="100%">

The default light is a dim, overcast morning. The hearths, candles, lanterns and the forge light the rooms around them. A **Dusk ↔ Day** slider runs from full daylight down to dusk, when the cressets at the gates are lit.

### Old, and lived in

Damp creeps up the wall feet, with rain streaks and lichen above it. Moss grows on the slates, and the flagstones are worn and cracked. Grass grows in the joints of the wall-walk, and there are putlog holes, spouts and scaffolding. Beyond the walls are gorse, russet bracken, wind-bent thorn trees, a rock-cut ditch with standing water, a rutted cart track and a hurdle sheepfold.

## A real castle: Las Aguzaderas, c. 1410

![Las Aguzaderas cut through its keep, with the courtyard, the wall stair and the albarrana tower beyond](docs/images/aguzaderas.jpg)

Aberwen is invented. Las Aguzaderas is not: it still stands in a shallow hollow near El Coronil, on what was the frontier with the Nasrid kingdom of Granada. Unusually, it was built low down, not on a height, because its job was to hold a spring. The model sticks to what is likely to be accurate:

- **The plan is measured** from OpenStreetMap and the PNOA orthophoto: a near-square courtyard about 30 × 32 m, square towers at three corners, half-round towers on the east and west walls, and the square keep straddling the middle of the south wall.
- **The towers and keep follow the published descriptions.** The corner towers are solid to the wall-walk, with one chamber under a pointed barrel vault and a ladder to the roof. The keep sits on a rubble podium, with two storeys under sail vaults, a machicolation over its door, corner bartizans and a small signal turret on the terrace.
- **The albarrana tower** at the north-east stands out from the corner, joined by a pointed arch, and holds the well. It is cut open, and the later low enclosure with rounded corners wraps round it.
- **What is not known is said to be unknown.** No measured section has been published online, so heights and wall thicknesses are estimates. Nothing is recorded of the garrison. The sources disagree about the gate, and the notes say so. The people (an alcaide, crossbowmen, a spearman with a leather adarga, a lookout, a lad and a herdsman with his cattle at the spring) are marked as conjecture.
- **One working mechanism:** the signal turret. Light it by day for smoke or towards dusk for a beacon fire, and the lookout turns to watch.

Every statement is cited: the IAPH heritage record, the 1923 monument order, local descriptions, Molina Rozalem's study of the sister tower at Lopera, and studies of frontier arms and fortification. [`SOURCES-AGUZADERAS.md`](SOURCES-AGUZADERAS.md) lists them, and [`docs/research/aguzaderas.md`](docs/research/aguzaderas.md) holds the full research, with a confidence mark on every claim.

## Getting it right

The castle is invented, but its parts are not. Every note in the side panel cites its sources: Arnold Taylor's *King's Works in Wales*, C. M. Woolgar's studies of the medieval household and its food, the 1318 Household Ordinance of Edward II, Walter of Bibbesworth on brewing, Peter Brears on cooking and dining, the Morgan Picture Bible, and others.

Where the evidence is thin or the scene has had to invent, the note says so with a **conjecture** mark. [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md) lists every reference and the claims it supports, along with every statement marked as conjecture (36 sources and 73 conjectures at present).

## Running it

```sh
npm install
npm run dev      # development server at http://localhost:5173
npm run build    # static production build in dist/ (relative paths; host it anywhere)
npm run preview  # serve the production build
```

### Controls

| | |
|---|---|
| **Orbit** | Drag. You can circle the whole castle; inside a room the view keeps the open side towards you. |
| **Pan** | Right-drag, shift-drag, or WASD / arrow keys (Q / E for down / up). |
| **Zoom** | Scroll; it zooms towards the cursor. |
| **Visit a room** | Click it in the view, the room list or the plan. The rest of the castle fades back. **Esc** or **Back** returns. |
| **Learn** | Hover anything for its name and a one-line gloss. Click a room, mechanism or person for notes and sources. |
| **Meet people** | Click a person to greet them; use **Follow** in the panel to keep the camera on them. **Esc** stops. |
| **Light** | Use the Dusk ↔ Day slider under the plan. |

### Checking a change

```sh
npm run check                          # build, render headless, screenshot, fail on any console error
npm run check -- --shots kitchen,hall  # screenshots of particular rooms (room ids)
npm run check -- --no-build --out DIR  # reuse the last build; choose the output folder
npm run sources                        # regenerate SOURCES.md from src/data/
```

`check` runs the build in headless Chromium with software WebGL. Its frame rate is not representative of a real GPU. For a frame-rate readout in the app, press **F** or open it with `?stats`.

## How it's made

Three.js and Vite, in plain JavaScript modules. There are no textures or models: every surface pattern is procedural, generated in the shaders.

- **Real sections.** `src/geom/builder.js` clips every convex solid against the cut plane and generates true cap faces, tagged as section fill. Coplanar T-junctions are welded so no cracks show. Boulders, bushes and trees in `src/geom/nature.js` are built the same way, so they cut cleanly too.
- **One shader, three targets.** The surface shader in `src/render/material.js` writes watercolour colour, view normals and a picking label in a single pass. It draws coursed masonry, slates, boards, flags, cobbles, wattle, painted false-ashlar and log ends, plus the weathering. It also handles daylight that dims indoors and up to 24 flickering fire lights.
- **Ink and paper.** A post pass in `src/render/post.js` finds edges from depth, normals and labels, and draws them as wobbling, dry-brushed ink. It then pools pigment at wash edges, granulates it into the paper tooth and prints the whole frame on paper. Smoke, steam, flames and light halos are watercolour particles (`src/render/fx.js`).
- **People.** The figures in `src/people/` are articulated, built from smooth pleated surfaces of revolution and driven by looping routines of walks and poses. `life.js` populates the wards and fields. `banners.js` sets up the flags, which the vertex shader ripples with smooth cloth normals.
- **The castle.** `src/castle/` holds the layout, the shell (curtains, towers, gatehouse, ranges, rock), the furnished rooms (`furnish.js`, `interiors.js`), the wards (`wards.js`), the landscape (`landscape.js`), and the fires and smoke (`ambient.js`).
- **Mechanisms** live in `src/mech/`. **Glossary, notes and sources** live in `src/data/`.

All code and art are original.
