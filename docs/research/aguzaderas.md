# Castillo de las Aguzaderas (El Coronil, Sevilla): fact sheet

Researched 2026-10-22. Each claim carries a confidence tag and a source key (list at the end):
- **[WA]** well attested (two or more independent sources agree)
- **[1S]** stated by one source
- **[UC]** uncertain or conflicting
- **[OBS]** my own measurement from OSM vector data or orthophotos. This is not a published source, so treat it as approximate.

> **Compass warning (read first).** The official IAPH description puts the keep on the *west* curtain, the semicircular towers on the *north and south* flanks, and the former albarrana tower at the *south-east*. Orthophotos (PNOA, Esri), OpenStreetMap, the La Casa de la Tercia blog and minube all put the keep on the **south** side and the semicircular towers on the **east and west** walls. Rotating every IAPH bearing 90° anticlockwise (IAPH N→true W, E→N, S→E, W→S) makes all of IAPH's statements match the imagery. On that reading, IAPH's "south-east" albarrana becomes the true **north-east** complex. **[UC — my reconciliation; it is internally consistent across four separate IAPH statements]**

## 1. Dating and history

| Claim | Tag | Src |
|---|---|---|
| The site had an earlier Muslim phase. The post-conquest *Repartimiento* names the place **"Aznalcázar"** beside the Aguzaderas spring and records it as given to the **Cabildo (chapter) of Seville Cathedral**. *Aznalcázar* ("hisn al-qasr") implies an earlier tower guarding the spring. No Almohad fabric has been identified. | 1S (IAPH), echoed by blog | IAPH, TERCIA |
| "Built by Berbers" is a vague attribution that the sources treat as doubtful. | UC | WP-ES |
| Main construction dates to the **mid-14th c.** The Cabildo carried out "important works" in **1348–1355**. IAPH gives the typological chronology as 1348–1355. | WA | IAPH, TERCIA, WP-ES |
| Alternative view: the keep belongs to a family of **Seville-council (concejo)** towers of the **first third of the 14th c.** (Águila, Lopera, Gandul), "almost mimetic" in design. | 1S (academic). Conflicts with the Cabildo attribution. | MOLINA-L |
| **1355**: the Crown takes it from the Cabildo in exchange for **6,000 maravedís a year**. | WA (IAPH; blog says "bought") | IAPH, TERCIA |
| **1388**: given to **Per Afán de Ribera "el Viejo"** (later Adelantado Mayor de Andalucía, 1396). Juan I swapped Per Afán's 6,000-mrs juro for the castle "because it lay on the frontier with the Moors". | WA | IAPH, PERAFAN |
| It later formed part of the señorío of El Coronil. Through marriage alliances it passed to the **House of Medinaceli**, which held it until the 19th c. No source links it to Ponce de León / Arcos or to Utrera. | WA / 1S | IAPH, TERCIA |
| In the 15th c. the inhabitants moved to El Coronil, a castle-village founded **1381**. | WA | WP-ES, PASCUAL |
| It **lost its military function in the early 16th c.**, when the garrison was withdrawn. | 1S | TERCIA |
| Later uses: **soap factory**, then a **cattle pen**, which damaged it. A woman, María Rodríguez, is said to be buried in the patio (fevers of 1800). | 1S each | IAPH; WP-ES (citing chronicler Garrido) |
| **Protection:** declared *Monumento arquitectónico-artístico* by Real Orden of **12 Feb 1923** (Gaceta de Madrid no. 48, **17 Feb 1923**), not 1931. It is now a BIC (Monumento) under Ley 16/1985 (BOE 29-06-1985), code RI-51-0000240. IAPH code 01410360043. | WA | GACETA, IAPH, WP-ES |
| **Restorations:** "deep" restoration in the **1960s** ("Ministerio de Cultura"; the blog says mid-20th c., "especially towers and merlons"). Note: that ministry only existed from 1977, so the agency name is anachronistic. | WA (date) / UC (agency) | WP-ES, TERCIA |
| **2023**: works were ongoing, directed by archaeologists P. J. Ramírez Moreno and M. Herrera Jiménez. 18th-c. masonry and plaster were found in the "South Tower" (= keep). The next phase covers the hall, the vault and the "original pointed stone arches". | 1S | UVITEL |
| **Ownership** is conflicting. One account: Medinaceli donated it to the town in the late 19th c. Another: the Ayuntamiento acquired it "recently". A third: the State (Ministerio de Hacienda) **ceded it to the Ayuntamiento in June 2023**. | UC | TERCIA, IAPH, UVITEL |
| **Name.** The popular explanation is a rocky place where boars "sharpened" (*aguzar*) their tusks. *Aguzadero* is a real hunting term for that. Philologist Pascual Barea finds this unconvincing. He proposes an original **"La Abuzadera"** (attested 1788 and 1795): a spring so low that one had to drink *abuzado*, lying face down. A third option is a place for whetting tools. "La Aguadera" is a 19th-c. reinterpretation. | UC | PASCUAL, TERCIA, MINUBE |

## 2. Site

- **It sits in a hollow (*vaguada*), not on a height**, because its job was to guard the **spring/fountain of Las Aguzaderas**, not to control territory. A ring of towers did the territorial watch: Cote, El Bollo, Lopera, Águila, Alocaz and Llado. **[WA]** WP-ES, IAPH, TERCIA, MINUBE
- Terrain **[OBS, Copernicus DEM via Open-Meteo]**: the castle stands at about 119–127 m (BURGEN gives 119 m). Ground rises to about 136–160 m 200–400 m to the S/SE (toward the Sierra de Montellano). It is about 130–135 m to the N and E, and falls to about 114–118 m 400 m to the W. It is therefore a shallow valley draining roughly west. Low hills block distant views. **[1S]** TERCIA says this is why a signal turret was put on the keep.
- **Where the water was:** IAPH says the albarrana tower "enclosed a **well** (*pozo*)" to secure water. The blog says the albarrana "was the protection of the spring" and that a later **parapet-less wall** was built round the fountain area. On the compass reconciliation above, that puts the spring/well at the **NE complex**. **[UC]** There is no surveyed position of the present-day spring. The blog notes "numerous **troughs (*pilones*)** where livestock watered" in the surroundings. **[1S]**
- **Land use in the 14th–15th c.:** the sources speak of "rich lands" and a coveted spring (PASCUAL) and call the estate a *donadío* (WP-ES). Frontier fortlets sheltered the few settlers **and their herds (*hatos de ganado*)** (MOLINA-L, a general statement). No document found specifies cereal, olives or pasture for Aguzaderas. **[gap]**
- **Roads:** today it is reached by a 400 m track from the El Coronil–Montellano road (A-375), about 3 km from El Coronil. **[WA]** IAPH, WP-ES, BURGEN. The road along the west/north-west side in your satellite view is the local *Camino de la Carrascosa*. The A-375 passes about 480 m to the east **[OBS, OSM]**. The medieval route is not documented. **[gap]**

## 3. Plan and dimensions

**Published descriptions**
- **Quadrangular / "almost square" plan, square towers at the corners, two semicircular towers mid-flank, plus the keep.** **[WA]** IAPH, TERCIA, MINUBE, TURISMO
- **Keep in the middle of the S wall, square, projecting beyond the curtain** ("atypical because it sticks out of the wall"). **[WA]** TERCIA, MINUBE, and IAPH rotated.
- **Semicircular towers mid-way along the E and W walls.** **[WA]** TERCIA, OSM, imagery. IAPH says N and S (see the compass warning).
- **Fourth corner:** only three corners have true corner towers. At the fourth, a square tower "not exactly at the corner" is joined to the circuit **by a pointed arch**. It was **originally an albarrana tower** enclosing the well. **Early in the 15th c.(?)** a **quadrangular enclosure** was added around it, which turned the arch into the gate of that new space. **[WA as a feature; position UC]** IAPH. The blog puts the tower and enclosure on the **west wall's** northward extension, and mentions a "**blocked arch under the albarrana tower**". The imagery shows the walled annex at the **NE**.
- **Corner towers:** solid up to wall-walk level. Above that is one chamber with a **pointed barrel vault**, arrow-slits (*saeteras*) on all four faces, and a hatch in a corner of the vault for a ladder up to a **crenellated roof terrace**. Wooden ladders were used, with no masonry stairs. The semicircular towers have their stair **in the centre**. **[WA]** IAPH, TERCIA
- **Wall-walk:** reached by a stair built against the "north angle" (IAPH bearing) of the curtain. The adarve is the only route to the towers. **[1S]** IAPH
- **Keep, two vaulted storeys plus terrace.** Ashlar at corners, plinth and door. The lower storey is **not solid**: it has a chamber under a **bóveda vaída** (sail vault), and the upper storey has the same. Originally there were **bartizans (*garitas*) on corbels at all four roof corners**; only stubs survive. **[WA]** IAPH, TERCIA ("escaragüitas")
- **Keep entrance:** door with a "curious lintel", with remains of a **machicolation (*matacán*)** above at upper-floor level. It leads to a vaulted ground room with a **small annexe room**. The upper floor overlooks that annexe through a window and a **murder-hole (*buhera*)**. The passage to the stair had its own door. A **narrow stair** runs to the terrace. **[1S]** TERCIA. Molina Rozalem confirms the Aguzaderas keep door is well preserved, in ashlar with **double-bocel corbels carrying the lintel**. He also says the keep stands on a **rubble podium about 1 m high** and has vertical defences (machicolations at windows or upper corners) that Lopera lacks. **[1S academic]** MOLINA-L
- **Signal turret:** a small turret about **3 m tall** (*specula*) on the keep terrace was used to signal to El Coronil's castle. **[1S]** TERCIA
- **Main gate.** One source puts it on the **W wall**, a **pointed arch** with remains of a **machicolation** above (TERCIA). Another says it is on the side **without** a semicircular tower, **opposite the keep**, i.e. the N side, "with stair and portcullis (*rastrillo*)" (MINUBE). An unverified search snippet mentions a *ladronera* and two flanking towers. **[UC]** No source describes a bent entrance or a gate-tower.
- **Barbican / second line:** none recorded except the NE (or W) annex around the albarrana/well. **[1S]**
- **Internal buildings:** none described. The patio is bare, the floor "lifted" down to rock (MINUBE). IAPH has photos titled "vista parcial del patio" but gives no description. **[gap]**

**Measured from OSM (way 478142381 and tower parts) and PNOA orthophoto [OBS]**
- Overall footprint about **1,620 m²**, matching "over 1,600 m²" in UVITEL. Bounding box about 54 × 54 m including projections.
- The enclosure is rotated about **18–20° clockwise** from north: the W and E walls run at bearing about 018°, the S wall about 108°, the N wall about 101°. The plan is slightly trapezoidal.
- Corner-tower centre to centre: W side about 37 m, S about 36 m, E about 42 m, N about 36.5 m. The curtain runs are about 31–37 m, so the courtyard is roughly 30 × 32 m.
- **Keep** about **11 × 11 m**, projecting about 5.5 m south of the S curtain, i.e. half in, half out. Its terrace shows a merloned parapet and a small round feature about 2–2.5 m across, probably the specula or stair head.
- **Corner towers** about **5 × 5 m** (SW, SE, NW, plus the small NE one). **Semicircular towers** about **4 m wide × 3–4 m deep** (E-wall one clearly D-shaped; W-wall one tagged in OSM).
- **NE annex** about **13 × 10 m** outside the N curtain at its E end. Its outer walls have **no merlons** and **rounded corners**, which matches TERCIA's "wall without parapet". From the ground, a rounded corner of this wall could read as a "ruined round tower".
- **Merlons** in the imagery are **rectangular, square-topped** with regular crenels along every curtain and tower. Note they were heavily restored in the 1960s. **[UC for medieval shape]**
- Wall-walk plus parapet width is about 1.5–2 m from the imagery. **[OBS, rough]**

**Gaps:** wall heights and thicknesses, tower heights, keep height and floor heights, gate dimensions, depth of the well, any cistern. I found no published measured section.

**Reconciling your images [UC, my reading]:** the large hollow square "tower" at the NE is the **added enclosure round the former albarrana tower/well**, not the keep. The protruding square tower in the middle of the south side is the **keep (torre del homenaje)**. Your ground photo fits a view **from the east**. Left to right: SE corner tower; the keep behind and taller; the E-wall semicircular cubo; the E curtain; the tall NE (ex-albarrana) tower; then the rounded corner of the annex wall ("ruined round tower"). The blocked arch would be the albarrana arch (TERCIA photo "Arco cegado bajo la torre albarrana") or another blocked opening. Unconfirmed.

## 4. Materials and construction

- **Rubble masonry (*mampostería*) curtains, ashlar quoins.** **[WA]** TERCIA, IAPH (keep: ashlar corners, plinth, door)
- The keep sits on a rubble podium about 1 m high. **[1S]** MOLINA-L
- **Stonemason's marks** have been recorded on the ashlar (J. L. González). This confirms dressed stone. The page was archived but not retrievable here. **[1S]** WP-ES external link
- **Vaults:** pointed barrel vaults in the towers and sail vaults in the keep **[WA]**. Their material (brick or stone) is not stated. **[gap]**
- **Roofs:** flat crenellated terraces (*azoteas*) on the towers and keep **[WA]**. No tiled roofs are recorded.
- **Mortar and stone colour:** not described. **[gap]** The imagery shows pale grey-ochre stone **[OBS]**. The 2023 works found 18th-c. plaster in the keep **[1S]** UVITEL.
- Regional context: 15th-c. frontier repairs used **lime (*cal*), *tapial*, brick, tile and rubble**. Gates were **wooden leaves sheathed in iron**, often renewed. **[1S, general, not Aguzaderas]** ROJAS

## 5. Garrison and life

- **Nothing specific** has been found on alcaides, garrison size or provisioning at Aguzaderas. **[gap]** The 1923 Gaceta, the Medinaceli archive (Fundación Casa Ducal de Medinaceli) and Mora-Figueroa 1973 would be the places to look.
- **What it guarded:** water, for settlers and stock, and denial of the spring to raiders from Ronda. **[WA]** IAPH, TERCIA, WP-ES. Frontier towers also gave **refuge to settlers and their herds** **[1S general]** MOLINA-L. Raiding parties aimed at cattle-rustling, crop-burning and captive-taking, met by small garrisons **[1S general]** ROJAS.
- **Equipment (analogues, not Seville-specific):**
  - Murcia, early 15th c. (cuantía riders): "hojas cubiertas de cuero y adarga, bacinete y lanza", i.e. leather-covered plates, leather shield, helmet and lance. **[1S]** TORRES
  - Order of Santiago, 1440: horse "e armas de la gineta, hojas e adarga e barreta, lança e espada". Richer men: **ballestas de polea** or **ballesta de pie**. Poorer men: a shield. Young men: **lances and darts**. **[1S]** SOLERA
  - Adargas were made of leather, and foot soldiers used the large *pavés* **[1S]** LEVA.
  - The troop types of the period were **caballeros, ballesteros and lanceros** **[WA]** TORRES, LEVA.
- **Animals:** cattle were kept in the castle in modern times **[1S]** IAPH. Herds sheltered at frontier towers **[1S general]** MOLINA-L. Watering troughs stand nearby **[1S]** TERCIA. Wild boar (*aguzadero*) appear in the name legend.

## 6. Published plans

- **Molina Rozalem, *Fortificaciones de la Banda Morisca en la provincia de Sevilla* (Diputación de Sevilla, 2016)** analyses Aguzaderas in full, with the author's own **elevation and plan drawings** (per the Atrio review). It is the best target; not online. **[1S]** ATRIO
- **Mora-Figueroa, "El castillo de las Aguzaderas", *Castillos de España* (AEAC) 1973, pp. 25–33** is likely to contain a planta. Also Lampérez (1923) and Hernández Díaz et al., *Catálogo arqueológico y artístico de la provincia de Sevilla* (1939). None were retrievable. **[gap]**
- IAPH holds 9 photos (gate and tower, patio, tower detail) but no plan. The blog has location maps only. No plan was found on Commons (9 photos). **[gap]**
- A plan has to be built from the OSM and PNOA geometry in §3. Analogue for the keep's interior: Torre Lopera, 12.90 × 11.30 m, walls 2.30–2.40 m thick with a 3.40 m stair wall, lower and upper vaulted chambers and terrace, door on the S face (MOLINA-L).
